How to read the tiers

Classification key
Confirmed

Documented fact

Established by declassified records, official admissions, court judgments or congressional investigation.

Studied

Documented, but reviewed

The program is real and on record, but its findings, scope or conclusions are disputed, partially redacted or under official review.

Unconfirmed

Claimed, no evidence

Widely alleged in public discourse. No credible public evidence supports the claim. Recorded here as claim, not fact.

Unknown

No record at all

No official documents, admissions or investigations exist either way. Rests entirely on testimony, books or folklore.

FILE 01
Confirmed
1953 — 1973
MKULTRA · subprojects 1–149

Project MKUltra

The agency's illegal human experimentation program for behavioral control — LSD, hypnosis, sensory isolation, electroshock and forced addiction — frequently tested on unwitting American citizens and foreign subjects.

  • 149 officially acknowledged subprojects run through universities, hospitals, prisons and clinics — many institutions never knew who was funding them.
  • Director Richard Helms ordered every file destroyed in 1973; surviving financial ledgers surfaced in 1977, forcing a Senate hearing (July 25–26, 1977).
  • Named subjects include Frank Olson (dosed, died 1953), Whitey Bulger, Ken Kesey and Ted Kaczynski.
Sources

  2. CIA FOIA Reading Room, MKULTRA release — cia.gov/readingroom/document/06760269
  3. National Security Archive, "The Top Secret Testimony of CIA's MKULTRA Chief" — nsarchive.gwu.edu
  4. John Marks, The Search for the "Manchurian Candidate" (Times Books, 1979).
  5. Wikipedia, "MKUltra" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKUltra
FILE 02
Confirmed
c. 1950 — 1976
Operation Mockingbird

Operation Mockingbird

The CIA's network of journalists, editors and news organizations — used to plant stories, shape coverage and, at times, pay reporters directly, at home and abroad.

  • The Church Committee was told in 1976 that the Agency had relationships with about 400 journalists; some were witting, some were paid assets.
  • CIA stated the domestic media program ended in March 1976; foreign media operations were said to continue.
  • Carl Bernstein's 1975 exposé detailed ties reaching The Washington Post, Time, Newsweek, CBS and others.
Sources
  1. U.S. Senate, Final Report of the Select Committee to Study Governmental Operations (Church Committee), Book I, 1976 — "Foreign and Domestic Intelligence Activities".

  4. CIA FOIA Reading Room — cia.gov/readingroom
FILE 03
Confirmed
1967 — 1974
Operation CHAOS · Project RESISTANCE

Operation CHAOS

A domestic espionage program that built extensive files on American antiwar, civil-rights and New Left organizations — work the CIA was legally barred from doing.

  • Created under Richard Helms in 1967; produced thousands of intelligence reports on U.S. citizens and groups.
  • Revelations helped trigger the 1975 Church Committee and the intelligence-reform laws that followed.
  • CIA's own Inspector General later reviewed and faulted the program's domestic reach.
Sources
  1. Church Committee, Final Report, 1976 — domestic intelligence chapters.
  2. CIA Inspector General, Report on CIA Activities Within the United States (declassified).
  3. Wikipedia, "Operation CHAOS" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_CHAOS
  4. CIA FOIA Reading Room — search "CHAOS"
FILE 04
Confirmed
1954 — 1966
Operation Midnight Climax

Operation Midnight Climax

CIA-funded brothels in San Francisco and New York where subjects were dosed with LSD and observed through two-way mirrors by agency staff.

  • Run by OSS veteran George Hunter White under Sidney Gottlieb's Technical Services branch — described internally as a "service substitution".
  • Established the safe-house method later used across MKUltra; taxpayer money paid the prostitutes and the booze.
  • Surfaced through the Church Committee and the CIA's 1973 "Family Jewels" compilation.
Sources
  1. U.S. Senate, Church Committee, Final Report, 1976.
  2. CIA "Family Jewels" release, CIA FOIA Reading Room — cia.gov/readingroom
  3. Wikipedia, "Operation Midnight Climax" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Midnight_Climax
FILE 05
Confirmed
1951 — 1957
Project ARTICHOKE · successor to BLUEBIRD

Project ARTICHOKE

The interrogation-focused forerunner to MKUltra: hypnosis, extreme drug protocols and forced addiction used to test whether a subject could be broken, conditioned or made to act.

  • Declassified memos discuss using the techniques against Soviet intelligence and for "special interrogation".
  • Evolved directly into MKUltra in 1953; the two programs share personnel, budgets and methods.
  • Surviving documentation is heavily redacted — the full operational record was never recovered.
Sources
  1. CIA FOIA Reading Room, declassified ARTICHOKE memoranda — cia.gov/readingroom
  2. John Marks, The Search for the "Manchurian Candidate" (1979).
  3. Wikipedia, "Project ARTICHOKE" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_ARTICHOKE
FILE 06
Confirmed
1946 — 1948
Guatemala STD experiments

Guatemala Experiments

U.S. Public Health Service doctors, working with Guatemalan officials and CIA-linked contacts, deliberately infected prisoners, soldiers, sex workers and psychiatric patients with syphilis and gonorrhea without consent.

  • Roughly 1,500 people were subjected to the experiments; hundreds were deliberately infected.
  • Concealed until 2010, when historian Susan Reverby uncovered the records; the U.S. formally apologized on October 1, 2010.
  • Run by the same researchers and era as Tuskegee — a companion crime to the better-known study.
Sources

  3. Rodriguez & Kreager, "First, Do No Harm," 2013 — PMC3828982
  4. Origins (OSU), "The Guatemala Inoculation Experiments" — origins.osu.edu
FILE 07
Confirmed
1953
Operation TPAJAX (US) · Operation Boot (UK)

Iran Coup — TPAJAX

The joint CIA–MI6 operation that overthrew Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh and restored the Shah — the template for Cold War regime change.

  • CIA funds moved through parliamentarians, newspapers and hired crowds; Kermit Roosevelt coordinated on the ground.
  • The first attempt (Aug. 15) collapsed; a second push succeeded on August 19, 1953.
  • Documented through declassified State Department volumes, a 1979 courtroom release and a CIA historical review declassified in 2013.
Sources
  1. U.S. Dept. of State, Foreign Relations of the United States, 1952–1954, Vol. X — Iran, 1951–1954 — history.state.gov
  2. CIA, declassified review of the 1953 coup ("The Battle for Iran"), released 2013 — CIA FOIA Reading Room
  3. Wikipedia, "1953 Iranian coup d'état" — en.wikipedia.org
FILE 08
Confirmed
2001 — 2013+
Rendition · Black sites · EIT

Extraordinary Rendition & Black Sites

The post-9/11 program of kidnapping detainees on foreign soil, holding them in secret prisons, and transferring them to jurisdictions where torture was permitted.

  • The Senate Intelligence Committee's 2014 study documented 119 detainees, secret facilities in Poland, Romania, Lithuania, Thailand and Afghanistan, and systematic misreporting to Congress and the White House.
  • Courts later confirmed individual cases: El-Masri v. Macedonia (ECHR, 2012), Nasr & Ghali v. Italy (2016), Zubaydah v. Poland (2014).
  • The program's existence, cost and detention roster are all now admitted on the official record.
Sources
  1. U.S. Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, Committee Study of the CIA's Detention and Interrogation Program, 2014 (full text declassified 2016) — intelligence.senate.gov
  2. DOJ Office of Inspector General, Special Review: CIA Detention and Interrogation Program, 2009 — justice.gov/oig
  3. European Court of Human Rights, El-Masri v. Macedonia, Grand Chamber judgment, Dec. 13, 2012.
  4. Wikipedia, "Extraordinary rendition" / "Black site" — en.wikipedia.org
FILE 09
Studied
1978 — 1995
GRILL FLAME · SCANATE · STARGATE

Project Stargate

The Pentagon and CIA's psychic-espionage program: "remote viewing" protocols, mostly run through Stanford Research Institute, intended to see distant or classified targets with the mind.

  • Real, funded and documented — including hundreds of experimental transcripts now declassified.
  • A 1995 review by the American Institutes for Research concluded the results had no proven intelligence value; CIA declined to renew it.
  • Classed as "studied" because official reviews existed and reached negative conclusions, while debate over individual cases continues.
Sources
  1. American Institutes for Research, An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications, 1995.
  2. CIA FOIA Reading Room, declassified remote-viewing files — cia.gov/readingroom
  3. Wikipedia, "Project Stargate" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Stargate
FILE 10

1962
Operation Northwoods

Operation Northwoods

Draft proposals by the Joint Chiefs of Staff for false-flag attacks on U.S. soil — hijackings, bombings, a downed airliner — to be blamed on Cuba to manufacture a case for war.

  • Documented in declassified 1962 memos: never approved and never implemented; rejected by Secretary McNamara and President Kennedy.
  • Publicized after declassification in 1997 and detailed in James Bamford's Body of Secrets.
  • Classed as "studied": the proposal is real and official, but its rejection is equally on the record.
Sources

FILE 11

c. 1948 — 1975
MKNAOMI · successor to MKCHART

MKNAOMI

The weapons side of the mind-control era: shellfish toxins, biological agents and concealment gadgets — including the infamous dart projector disclosed at the 1975 hearings.

  • CIA disclosed a poisoned-dart weapon designed to leave a lethal, traceless heart attack; a Church Committee exhibit made it infamous.
  • In January 1973, CIA officer Sidney Gottlieb ordered most of the toxin stockpile destroyed — later admitted to investigators.
  • Classed as "studied": the core facts are confirmed, but key records remain redacted and incomplete.
Sources

FILE 12
Studied
1974
Project Azorian · Glomar Explorer

Project Azorian

The secret mission to raise the sunken Soviet submarine K-129 from 16,000 feet of Pacific Ocean, hidden behind a cover story about Howard Hughes' mining ship.

  • One of the most expensive covert operations ever mounted — the custom ship Glomar Explorer was built for it.
  • Partially acknowledged: the CIA's own internal history was released through FOIA, while other details stay withheld — giving us the "neither confirm nor deny" Glomar response.
  • Classed as "studied" because the operation is documented yet incompletely disclosed.
Sources
  1. CIA, Project Azorian: The Story of the Hughes Glomar Explorer (declassified) — CIA FOIA Reading Room

FILE 13

1981 — 1998
"Dark Alliance" allegations

Contra–Cocaine Allegations

Claims that the CIA tolerated or enabled Nicaraguan Contra drug trafficking to fund the anti-Sandinista war — triggered into the open by Gary Webb's 1996 series.

  • Webb's "Dark Alliance" prompted two official 1998 investigations: DOJ's Inspector General and the CIA's Inspector General.
  • Findings: no evidence the CIA directed trafficking — but documented failures to act on knowledge of Contra-linked traffickers and under-reporting to the DEA.
  • Classed as "studied": officially investigated with mixed, contested conclusions.
Sources

FILE 14
Studied
1963 — 1998
Warren · Church · HSCA · ARRB

JFK Assassination Investigations

Decades of official inquiry into whether intelligence agencies were involved in Kennedy's murder — including reviews of CIA conduct, Oswald's contacts and withheld files.

  • Warren Commission (1964): lone-gunman conclusion, later criticized for reliance on incomplete intelligence.
  • HSCA (1979): found a probable conspiracy but did not identify a CIA role — while faulting the CIA and FBI for withholding information.
  • The Assassination Records Review Board (1993–98) declassified millions of pages; a set of records remains embargoed by presidential order.
Sources
  1. U.S. National Archives, JFK assassination records — archives.gov/research/jfk
  2. House Select Committee on Assassinations, Final Report, 1979.
  3. Wikipedia, "House Select Committee on Assassinations" — en.wikipedia.org

"MKUltra never ended"

Post-1973 MKUltra Continuation

The claim that behavioral-control research continued under other names after Helms destroyed the files — that 1973 was a cover, not an ending.

  • Claimed basis: destroyed records, references to successor designations in documents, and surviving personnel.
  • No public evidence has surfaced showing an operational successor program; the CIA states behavioral research ended.
  • Recorded as a claim: the destroyed files are fact, the continuation is not.

Unconfirmed
Claimed: 1976 → present
"Mockingbird 2.0"

Modern Media Control Claims

The assertion that Mockingbird-style control of domestic media — now extended to platforms and influencers — never really stopped.

  • What is confirmed: paid journalist relationships existed until the CIA's 1976 statement, and later disclosures documented FBI–platform communications (2022 "Twitter Files").
  • What is not confirmed: no public evidence of current CIA payments to U.S. journalists or coverage-directing.
  • Marked unconfirmed — the historical program is fact, the modern continuation is inference.
Sources
  1. Wikipedia, "Operation Mockingbird" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mockingbird
  2. Church Committee, Final Report, 1976 (basis for the 1976 end-date).
  3. "Twitter Files" publications, 2022–23 (documented FBI–platform contact; not CIA media payments).

"CIA orchestration of 9/11"

9/11 Orchestration Claims

The claim that the CIA planned or permitted the September 11 attacks. No credible public evidence supports it; official investigations attribute the attacks to al-Qaeda.

  • What is confirmed: CIA tracked two future hijackers (al-Mihdhar & al-Hazmi) and failed to notify the FBI — criticized in the 2002 Joint Inquiry and the CIA's own 2005 review.
  • A failure of information-sharing is documented; orchestration is not.
  • Marked unconfirmed to keep that distinction visible.

Crash-Retrieval & Reverse-Engineering Claims

Allegations that recovered non-human craft and materials are held by defense-intelligence contractors under secret programs — from Bob Lazar's 1989 claims to later whistleblower testimony.

  • Confirmed nearby facts: AATIP (2007–2012) was a real Pentagon UAP study, and legitimate UAP reporting channels now exist.

FILE 21
Confirmed
1959 — 1973 · released 2007
The Family Jewels

The Family Jewels

The CIA's own inventory of "potentially illegal, inappropriate and otherwise sensitive" activities — commissioned by Director Schlesinger in 1973 after Watergate and assembled by William Colby as "the skeletons in the CIA's closet".

  • Covers mail opening, break-ins, surveillance of journalists, interrogation abuses and domestic drug work — the Agency admitting its own conduct in writing.
  • Most of the collection was released on June 25, 2007, after a 15-year FOIA campaign by the National Security Archive.
  • Provided the raw material for the 1975 Church Committee hearings that produced Volume I of this archive.
Sources
  1. Wikipedia, "Family Jewels (Central Intelligence Agency)" — en.wikipedia.org
  2. National Security Archive, Family Jewels FOIA campaign — nsarchive.gwu.edu
  3. CIA FOIA Reading Room, Family Jewels release — cia.gov/readingroom
FILE 22
Confirmed
Jun — Aug 1954
Operation PBSUCCESS

Operation PBSUCCESS

The CIA's covert overthrow of Guatemala's elected government — propaganda radio, armed proxies and CIA air power — ten months after the Iran coup that gave the playbook its first success.

  • Followed TPAJAX (FILE 07) at scale: forged documents, black propaganda, a mercenary force and psychological operations against President Árbenz.
  • CIA released its operational history of the coup in 1999 after years of FOIA pressure — a rare case of the Agency publishing its own regime-change record.
  • Installed Carlos Castillo Armas; Guatemala then lived under military-backed rule and civil war for a generation.
Sources
  1. Wikipedia, "1954 Guatemalan coup d'état" (Operation PBSUCCESS) — en.wikipedia.org
  2. CIA historical release on the 1954 coup, 1999 — CIA FOIA Reading Room
  3. National Security Archive, Guatemala documentation — nsarchive.gwu.edu
FILE 23
Confirmed
1960 — 1961 · disclosed 1975
CIA–Mafia plots

CIA–Mafia Assassination Plots

The CIA's contract with Mafia bosses — Santos Trafficante, Sam Giancana, and intermediaries reaching Lucky Luciano — to poison Fidel Castro with pills, cigars and coffee.

  • Organized by CIA officer William Harvey using underworld intermediaries; the Mafia's price was help getting back into Cuban casinos.
  • Disclosed by the Church Committee in February 1975 — the report that first made the Agency's assassination reach public.
  • Two of the middlemen (Roselli, Giancana) were murdered before they could testify.
Sources
  1. U.S. Senate, CIA's Involvement in Assassination Plots Involving Foreign Leaders, Feb. 18, 1975.
  2. Wikipedia, "CIA assassination attempts on Fidel Castro" — en.wikipedia.org
  3. National Security Archive, Church Committee records — nsarchive.gwu.edu
FILE 24
Confirmed
1961 — 1962
Operation Mongoose · "Cuban Project"

Operation Mongoose

The campaign to wreck Cuba after the Bay of Pigs: sabotage of refineries and sugar harvests, harassment, demolitions and repeated attempts on Castro's life — run from Washington by General Lansdale with CIA officers.

  • Directed through the "Cuban Project" with declassified memoranda — including the October 4, 1962 operations meeting record.
  • Overlap with FILE 23: poison pills, cigars, scuba-gear ambushes — the same networks, now formally tasked.
  • Wound down after the Cuban Missile Crisis; its paper trail survives in declassified memos and the Foreign Relations series.
Sources

  2. National Security Archive, Cuba/Bay of Pigs documentation — nsarchive.gwu.edu
  3. U.S. Dept. of State, Foreign Relations of the United States, 1961–1963, Vol. X, Cuba.

Frank Olson

§ 05

Method & limits

Read before citing
Tiering rule

An entry is confirmed only when a primary record, court judgment or official admission exists. A program with official review but contested or incomplete records is studied. Claims with no evidence are unconfirmed; claims with no record at all are unknown.

Victim status

Confirmed subjects are established in documents or judgments. Disputed means the person is documented but the CIA link is contested. Alleged entries — like Terry Davis — are public claims without supporting evidence, shown for completeness, not endorsed.

Sourcing

Prefer primary sources: declassified FOIA material, Senate reports, court rulings and official investigations. Wikipedia links are navigation aids, not authorities. Every entry lists its own citations; verify before repeating anything here.

What this is not

This is an evidence map, not an indictment of anything ongoing. Where the record shows wrongdoing, it says so; where a claim is only a claim, it says that too. Absence of evidence is labeled as absence — nothing more.

Connections policy

The links log tiers company and celebrity ties by the same rule: contracts, filings and declassified records are confirmed; press-reported partial ties are studied; ownership/control claims without evidence (Rothschild, BlackRock, Epstein) are unconfirmed — and are logged as claims, not findings.

About this archive

An independent, educational index of declassified and well-documented intelligence history. Not affiliated with, endorsed by or operated by any government, agency or corporation. Compiled from open sources only — no classified material is reproduced, and every entry names its sources so you can check it yourself.
